## 1. What This Mod Does

Salt is the backbone of preservation, and in vanilla it runs out. This mod hides **15 natural salt licks** in the woods of Kentucky. Dig the salty soil and leach it into brine in a wooden vat, or draw weak brine straight from a **brine spring**; boil the brine down and scrape off **Evaporated Salt**. It's renewable forever.

## 2. Quick Start

> **Requires Traditional Preservation (Core)**, the series' shared mod (TP Core for short). Enable it alongside this one; it loads first.

| Step | You need | You get |
|---|---|---|
| Dig Saline Soil | Shovel + empty sack | Sack of Saline Soil (5 kg) |
| *or* Fill with Spring Brine | any empty container, at a spring | Spring brine: a 10 L bucket is 1 L Brine (boils to 1 salt) |
| Prepare Leaching Charge | Soil sack + metal bucket full of water (clean or tainted) or brine | Leaching Charge (sack returned) |
| Leach Brine | **Leaching Vat** (Carpentry 3) | a bucket of brine (5 L of Brine from water; a stronger liquid gains less), in 2 hours |
| Boil | the bucket of brine into a fire, stove or oven | Bucket of Salt Crust, in about 4 h on a fire, about 2 h in a hot oven |
| Scrape Salt Crust | Knife or cleaver (kept) | **1 Evaporated Salt per litre of Brine** (5 from one pass) + empty bucket |
| *or* Boil in a **salt pan** | a Salt Pan fitted on TP Core's Stone Hearth; any brine poured in, up to 30 L | a crust to scrape: 1 salt per litre of Brine, for less wood |

One sack of soil makes **5 salt**; a bucket from a spring, **1**.

## 3. Finding a Salt Lick

**What it looks like:** 3 to 7 tiles on natural ground (never on roads or floors, under buildings or trees, or in water): a dark, uneven patch of mineral soil **flecked with white**, and beside it one or more **brine springs**, shallow blue-grey pools in a rim of salt. A lick of 3 or 4 tiles has 1 soil tile, one of 5 to 7 has 2 (a lick shaped like a plus sign has 1); the rest are springs, and every tile touches one of its own kind, so the soil and the pools each make one piece.

**Where:** 15 in the world, **one per region**, in woodland, most of them near water. **Every town has one within about 1,500 tiles.** Each appears within a minute of its ground loading.

**Found, rarely: the Salt Lick Map.** Rangers, campers and the odd surveyor kept note of the licks. A map turns up about as rarely as a Generator Magazine, in a ranger station's desk or magazine rack, a camping store's book shelf, a library, and (seldom) an office desk. Read it like any town map: it shows the woods around **the lick nearest where you found it**, with a pencil ring **120 tiles across**. The lick is inside the ring but not at its middle, so you still search. The ring stays on the paper: reading the map marks only a small patch of woods (100 by 100 tiles) on your world map, so keep the map to see the ring.

> *Margin note:* buffalo and deer wore trails to the licks long before anyone drew a map. Big Bone Lick in northern Kentucky was known to French explorers by 1739 and later boiled for salt, and licks gave their names to places all over the state: Blue Licks, Drennon's Lick, Mud Lick.

## 4. Walkthrough: Soil to Salt

**Dig Saline Soil.** Right-click a lick tile with a shovel and an empty sack. It takes 3 in-game minutes. Each tile holds **5 diggings** and regrows **1 a week** (sandbox), even while you're away. A worked-out tile turns **paler and loses its white flecks** until it regrows.

**Or Fill with Spring Brine.** Right-click a spring: no tools, about 1 in-game minute. It fills the container in your hand, else a metal bucket, else any empty container (or one already holding spring brine, to top it up). Spring brine is weak: a full bucket is **1 L of Brine** and 9 L of tainted water, so it drinks as tainted and boils to **1 salt**. A spring holds **30 L** and fills back **10 L a day** (scaled by the regrowth setting); drawn dry it shows mud until it refills. Hover the option to see how much is left.

**Prepare Leaching Charge.** Soil sack + a metal bucket holding 10 L of water. **Tainted water works:** the brine is boiled down afterwards, which kills germs. You get the sack back. A full bucket of **brine** works too, from the vat or a spring: run it through and it comes out stronger, though strong brine takes up less (under 5 L of Brine adds 5, 5-7 adds 3, 8-9 adds 2, to 10, saturated, which is refused). Water goes 0 → 5 → 8 → 10; spring brine 1 → 6 → 9 → 10.

**Build the Leaching Vat** (Carpentry 3): 6 planks, 12 nails, a cloth filter; hammer and saw kept. **Outdoors only**: leaching is wet, muddy work.

**Leach Brine.** Load the charge and press Craft. **2 in-game hours**, even while you're away. The vat shows soil and a bucket while working, then spent soil and a full bucket until you collect. Its window says how strong the brine will come out, and how long is left.

**Boil.** Put the bucket of brine into any fire, stove or oven: it becomes a **Bucket of Boiling Brine**, and boils once the heat is lit. A part-used bucket boils too, so long as it holds a litre of Brine. Take it off before it is done and it is a bucket of brine again, and the boil starts over. It burns fuel as normal and pauses if the fire goes out. You hear it boil, and in TP Core's Stone Hearth it shows as the pot set in the hole. Hover a bucket to see its strength: *Weak brine* (a spring's), *Brine*, *Strong brine* or *Saturated brine*, and the salt it boils to. Scraping gives **1 Evaporated Salt per litre of Brine** in the bucket: 1 from a spring, 5 from one pass, 8 from two, 10 from three.

**Scrape.** Knife or cleaver (kept, may wear slightly) → the salt above + the bucket that went in, plain or forged.

### The salt pan

A shallow pan of forged iron, about 70 cm across and a hand deep, bedded in clay on top of TP Core's **Stone Hearth**. It boils more brine for the wood than a bucket does, because the brine lies wide and shallow over the fire.

| Step | You need | You get |
|---|---|---|
| **Forge Salt Pan** at a forge | 2 Steel Sheets, 2 Charcoal; a ball-peen hammer and metalworking pliers or tongs (kept). Blacksmith 3 | A **Salt Pan**. 25 Blacksmithing XP |
| **Fit Salt Pan** (the Stone Hearth's menu, fire out) | The Salt Pan, 2 Clay; a mason's trowel (kept). About half an hour | The **Stone Hearth (Salt Pan)** |
| **Pour Brine into the Salt Pan** | Any container of brine: Brine and water only, a part-used one too | Up to **30 L** in the pan, any time, even while it boils |
| Light the fire | Fuel | To the boil in about half an hour, then **6 L an hour** boiled off; banked, a third of that |
| **Scrape the Salt Pan** (once it is dry) | A knife or meat cleaver (kept). About 10 minutes | **1 Evaporated Salt per litre of Brine** poured in |
| **Empty the Salt Pan** (while it holds brine) | Nothing | The brine poured away, **the salt in it lost** |

You learn Forge Salt Pan from *Everyday Smithing - May 1993*, the magazine that teaches the Forged Cauldron, or at Blacksmith 4 with Cooking 2, or by researching a pan you've found.

**Feed it.** The salt stays in the pan as the water goes, so pour more brine in as it boils down and the salt builds up until you scrape. A full pan of spring brine (three buckets) is about **5.5 hours** of fire for 3 salt; the same three buckets boiled one after another are about 12.

**It waits.** Boiled dry, the pan holds its crust, even on a lit fire: nothing burns. Scrape when you're ready. Fractions of a portion are lost at the scrape, as with a bucket.

**Coming off.** **Remove Salt Pan** gives the pan back but not the clay, and only with the fire out and the pan empty and cool (below 40 C). A hot pan says so: let it cool first. To take it off before it has boiled down, **Empty the Salt Pan** first: the brine goes, and the salt with it.

**Under the pan.** The fire box stays open, so a bucket of brine can boil in it at the same time. Stone Hearth Info shows the pan's litres, its salt, its heat and when it will be dry.

> *Margin note:* a kettle boils from its bottom, a pan from its whole width. The wider and shallower the brine, the more water a fire drives off: England's inland salt works boiled their brine in broad, shallow iron pans for the same reason.

## 5. Packaging & Shakers

| Recipe | In | Out |
|---|---|---|
| Make Bag of Salt | 10 Evaporated Salt + empty paper bag | Bag of Salt (10 portions, counts as salt) |
| Pack Box of Salt Bags | 5 full bags | Box (unpacks back to 5 bags) |
| **Refill Salt Shaker with Evaporated Salt** | Shaker (Empty) + 2 Evaporated Salt | A real vanilla Salt shaker |
| **Refill Salt Shaker from a Bag of Salt** | Shaker (Empty) + 10 of the bag's 50 uses | A real vanilla Salt shaker; a used-up bag gives its paper bag back |

**Weight savings:** 10 loose salt weigh 1.0, a bag 0.8, a box of 5 bags 3.5.

**Found, very rarely:** a full Bag of Salt can turn up on a restaurant kitchen's sauce and baking shelves, and (super rarely) in a house's kitchen dry food. It is the same bag you make: 50 uses, counts as salt.

> *Margin note:* why refill shakers? Vanilla **bread, pizza, pie dough, biscuits, guacamole and jarring** ask for the vanilla Salt shaker *itself*, not just any salt, so they refuse loose salt. A refilled shaker works in all of them.

## 7. Secrets & Hidden Features

- **Empty shakers drop.** Use up a vanilla Salt shaker by hand and a **Shaker (Empty)** stays behind. Keep them all. They're your bread and pickles forever.
- **Licks regrow while you're away.** Regrowth counts in real game time, even in unloaded chunks. Rotate between tiles: 5 diggings each. Springs refill the same way, a bucket a day.
- **Run spring brine through the vat.** A sack of soil with spring brine in place of water gives 6 salt for one boil, not 5: the spring's litre comes free. Spring brine boiled as it is costs a whole fire for one salt.
- **Part-used Evaporated Salt won't go into bags or shaker refills**, which need full portions. Use it for seasoning instead. A part-used **bag** refills shakers fine: it pays by the use.
- **Ovens are twice as fast** as campfires for boiling brine.
- **A salt pan and a bucket share one fire.** The pan sits on the Stone Hearth's top; the fire box below still takes a bucket of brine.

## 8. The Saltworker's Notes

*Written in a careful hand on the inside cover of a Kentucky almanac.*

### What a sack is worth

| Item | Seasoning uses | Weight | Weight per use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evaporated Salt | 5 | 0.1 | 0.020 |
| Vanilla Salt shaker | 10 | 0.2 | 0.020 |
| Bag of Salt | 50 | 0.8 | 0.016 |
| Box of 5 Bags | 250 | 3.5 | 0.014 |

| From the ground | Evaporated Salt | Uses | Half-weight panfish salted (4 uses each) | 4-weight walleye salted (28 uses each) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One sack of soil | 5 | 25 | 6 | under 1 |
| One bucket from a spring | 1 | 5 | 1 | under 1 |
| One soil tile (5 sacks) | 25 | 125 | 31 | 4 |
| A spring, full (3 buckets) | 3 | 15 | 3 | under 1 |
| A whole lick's first stock (1-2 soil tiles, 2-5 springs) | 31 to 65 | 155 to 325 | 38 to 81 | 5 to 11 |

> *Margin note:* the Fish & Meat mod salts by size: 2 uses for each quarter weight of the packed fish, about what a Volga salter uses. A half-weight panfish takes 4 and a smaller one 2; a 4.5-weight walleye, the biggest fish that goes in whole, takes 32; a 10-weight catfish, gutted and split, 32 a half, more than a Bag of Salt.

> *Margin note:* each week a soil tile regrows one sack (5 salt) and a spring gives back seven buckets (7 salt), so a lick gives 19 (3 tiles) to 45 (7 tiles) Evaporated Salt a week, forever. The real limit is firewood: every bucket takes about 4 hours on a fire, two planks' worth. The soil's salt costs 0.4 of a plank each, the spring's 2: the springs never run out, but they eat the woods, as the Kentucky works did. A salt pan nearly halves it: three spring buckets in about 5.5 hours, not 12.

### Why it works the way it does

- **Why leach the soil?** Salt dissolves in water and earth doesn't. Pour water through salty soil and it carries the salt out with it, leaving the dirt behind. A cloth filter in the vat keeps the mud out of the brine.
- **Why can the water be dirty?** The brine is boiled down to dry crystals. Nothing survives that, so tainted water makes clean salt.
- **Why boil instead of letting it dry?** Sun drying works in hot, dry climates. Kentucky is neither, so its early saltworks boiled their brine in kettles over wood fires, as you do.
- **Why do licks recover?** Groundwater keeps bringing dissolved minerals up to the surface, where they're left behind as the water evaporates. Dig a spot out and, in time, the salt comes back.
- **Why is spring brine so weak?** Kentucky's springs ran at about 2% salt, weaker than sea water: 300 to 400 gallons for a bushel. A bucket of it boils to one salt, which is why the works ran dozens of kettles at once, and why running the brine through salty earth first saves so much wood.

### Old names, old ways

- **Kentucky really did run on licks.** Bison and deer wore trails to the salt springs, and settlers followed them. **Bullitt's Lick**, south of Louisville, became Kentucky's first commercial saltworks in the late 1700s, with rows of iron kettles boiling brine day and night.
- **Big Bone Lick**, further north, is named for the mammoth and mastodon bones found there. Ice Age animals came to the salt too.

## 9. Tips

- **Base near a lick** if you plan to fish and pickle heavily. A lick, a vat and a fire make a salt works.
- **Carry several empty sacks.** A soil tile gives 5 sacks, and a lick has only 1 or 2 soil tiles among its 3 to 7 (chapter 3), so 5 to 10 sacks before it needs time to regrow. The rest of its tiles are brine springs: bring a bucket for those.
- **Boil in batches in an oven.** Several buckets cook at once.
- **Box your salt for long hauls.** It's the lightest way to move it.
- **Keep a salt pan by the springs.** Fill buckets at the spring, pour them in as the pan boils down, scrape once a day.

## 10. Troubleshooting

| Problem | Why |
|---|---|
| Can't dig | Tile worked out (pale), no shovel, no empty sack, or the sack has items in it |
| Brine won't cook | The heat source isn't lit and fuelled; or the bucket isn't a metal one, holds under a litre of Brine, or holds something besides brine and water |
| Refill/bag refuses salt | Salt is part-used. Only full Evaporated Salt counts |
| Can't pick up the vat | It's running or has something in it |
| Fit Salt Pan is greyed out | The fire is lit, something is already fitted, or you're missing the pan, 2 Clay or a mason's trowel (its note says which) |
| Pour Brine into the Salt Pan is greyed out | The pan is full (30 L), or you carry nothing holding Brine and water only |
| Remove Salt Pan is greyed out | The fire is lit, the pan holds brine (Empty the Salt Pan) or a crust (scrape it), or it is still hot: let it cool |
| No lick in sight | Licks only appear once their ground loads. Check the search table |

## 12. Appendix: The Tester's Toolkit

*For testers, modders and the curious. These commands exist only when the game is started in debug mode (Steam launch option `-debug`). Type them into the in-game **Command Console** and press Enter. `TPS.help()` lists them in the game.*

> **Warning:** They change your save directly, so try them on a test save, not a playthrough you care about.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `TPS.licks()` | Lists all 15 licks without giving away where they are: whether each has been placed, its soil tiles and diggings left and its springs and litres left, or for an unplaced one its attempt count. A lick places the first time its ground loads. Safe to show on a stream or screenshot. |
| `TPS.licks(true)` | The same, plus each lick's coordinates and distance, an unplaced one's target square, its region and the nearest town. |
| `TPS.tp(n)` | Teleports you to lick `n`: its first tile if placed, or its target square if not, so it places as you arrive. |
| `TPS.nearest()` | The nearest lick, with distance and compass direction. |
| `TPS.tile()` | The lick tile under you: a soil tile's diggings left and hours until the next one regrows, or a spring's litres and how fast it refills. |
| `TPS.tiles(n)` | For each tile of lick `n` (or the nearest placed one): whether its ground object is there, which edge sprite it uses, soil or spring with its diggings or litres, and every other object on that square in drawing order. Use it when a lick looks wrong or tiles seem missing. |
| `TPS.refill(n)` | Fills every tile of lick `n` (or the lick you're standing on): soil to 5 diggings, springs to 30 L. |
| `TPS.empty(n)` | Digs every soil tile of lick `n` (or the one you're on) out to 0 and draws its springs dry, to test the worked-out and dry looks and regrowth. |
| `TPS.regrow(hours)` | Winds every lick's regrowth clock forward. `TPS.regrow(168)` is one week: one digging per soil tile, a spring full again. |
| `TPS.seed()` | This save's lick seed and the regrowth setting. |
| `TPS.give(name, n)` | Spawns `n` of: `sack` (Sack of Saline Soil), `salt` (Evaporated Salt), `bag` (Bag of Salt), `map` (Salt Lick Map, which picks the lick nearest where you first read it), `bucket` or `forgedBucket` (both empty), `pan` (Salt Pan). `TPS.give()` lists them. |
| `TPS.brine(litres, clean, total, forged)` | A bucket with `litres` of Brine (default 1, a spring's), the rest tainted water (clean water with `clean` true); `total` litres for a part-used one; `forged` true for a Forged Bucket. |
| `TPS.buckets()` | The Brine in every bucket, charge, boiling bucket and crust you carry. |
| `TPS.kit(kind)` | What the salt pan takes: `"fit"` (the pan, 2 Clay and a trowel) or `"forge"` (the forging materials, tools and magazine). |
| `TPS.panInfo()` | The nearest salt pan: its fire, litres, Brine, salt, heat, and hours to dry on a lit fire. |
| `TPS.pan(l, brine, c)` | Sets that pan: `l` litres with `brine` of them Brine at `c` degrees (default 30, 3, 20); `TPS.pan(0, 5)` leaves a dry crust of 5 salt. |

Changes to tiles show on the ground within an in-game minute.

**Typical check of placement:** `TPS.licks(true)`, then `TPS.tp(3)` to an unplaced one, wait a moment, and run `TPS.licks()` again. It should now say PLACED with 3 to 7 tiles.

**Typical check of regrowth:** stand on a lick, `TPS.empty()`, `TPS.tile()`, then `TPS.regrow(168)` and `TPS.tile()` again. It should show 1 digging back.
